a day with twin

you've met the other kind of assistant.

The kind that needs your calendar and texts to be useful, then ships all of it to a server you'll never see. Twin skips that trade, keeping everything in a DuckDB file at ~/.twin/twin.duckdb, right on your Mac. No accounts, no sync, no cloud database. Here's an ordinary Tuesday with it, and exactly what does or doesn't leave your laptop.

Everyone who installs Twin gets their own brand-new twin.duckdb, created on their Mac the first time they run it. Nothing is shared, synced, or pooled between users, and the database itself never leaves your machine.

Voice, the digest and nudges never touch an AI provider at all, they're built and matched entirely on your Mac. When you chat, one request goes to the provider you picked with your message, your name, the current time, today's event titles, and a fuzzy summary like "spent money on food earlier this week". Amounts, balances, account numbers and raw SMS never leave.

and a few smaller things

small buddy. surprisingly handy.

That's the daily stuff. No dashboard, no onboarding flow, no 14-step tour, just a little bubble on your screen with a few more tricks up its sleeve.

one hotkey away

Hit ⌘ ⇧ space from anywhere and Twin pops up in a floating bubble. Hit it again and it goes away. No app to switch to.

a little bit weird

Twin itself is warm and easygoing. Want more flavor? Swap in a sly shadow-ghost, a hype-machine spark, or a sleepy moon ghost with one click. None of them write you five-paragraph essays with headers.

fully open source

Every line is on GitHub. Read it, fork it, rip out the parts you don't like, give it a new personality. It's plain Python, not a black box.

peeks at your screen (only when asked)

Ask "what am I looking at?" and Twin takes one quick screenshot, reads it on your Mac, and deletes it right away. The AI only ever gets a one-line description, never the image.

bring your own AI

Claude, GPT, Gemini or Grok. Paste a key from whichever you like and Twin talks to it directly. Your key lives in the macOS Keychain, not in some file.

get twin

download. drag. say hi.

Twin is a real Mac app. No terminal, no Python, no config files. Setup happens inside Twin and takes about a minute.

Twin for Mac

v0.1.0 · macOS 13 Ventura or later · Apple silicon · free and open source

One command: downloads the latest .dmg, mounts it, copies Twin into Applications, and clears the quarantine flag so Gatekeeper doesn't block the first launch.

curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/woustachemax/twin/main/scripts/install.sh | bash

Not keen on piping a script from the internet into bash? Fair enough, download the .dmg manually instead.

download Twin ↓ or build it from source

Twin ships ad-hoc signed, not notarized by Apple, so the first launch after a manual download gets blocked. Here's how to clear it:

  1. Open the downloaded .dmg and drag Twin into your Applications folder.
  2. Double-click Twin. macOS blocks it: "Twin can't be opened because Apple cannot check it for malicious software." Click Done.
  3. Open System Settings → Privacy & Security.
  4. Scroll down to the Security section. You'll see "Twin was blocked to protect your Mac" with an Open Anyway button next to it.
  5. Click Open Anyway, then confirm Open in the dialog that follows (Touch ID or your password may be requested).

Or skip all of this with xattr -cr /Applications/Twin.app in Terminal after dragging it in.

01

get it onto your Mac

Run the quick install command above, or drag the manually downloaded .dmg into Applications. Either way, Twin.app ends up in your Applications folder.

02

first launch

Quick install already cleared Gatekeeper for you, so it just opens. Downloaded manually? See the Open Anyway steps above the first time you launch it.

03

set it up in the bubble

Pick your AI provider, paste your API key (it goes straight into your Keychain), tell Twin your name, and pick a buddy. Done.
